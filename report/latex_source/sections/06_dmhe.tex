\chapter{Distributed Moving-Horizon Estimation}
\label{ch:dmhe}

Each agent estimates its own state with the moving-horizon estimator of
\cref{sec:mhe}. This chapter couples those local estimators into a
\emph{distributed} estimator (DMHE) in which agents exchange information over the
communication graph so that the cluster localises better than any agent could
alone---and, in particular, so that an agent whose absolute-positioning
measurements are lost stays localised by leaning on its neighbours.

\section{Cluster measurement model}

Agent $i$ has access to two kinds of measurement. \emph{Absolute} measurements
relate its state to the inertial frame; the canonical example is a GNSS position
fix,
\begin{equation}
\vv{y}_i^{\text{abs}}=\vv{p}_i+\vv{n}_i^{\text{abs}},\qquad
\vv{n}_i^{\text{abs}}\sim\mathcal N(\vv{0},\mat{R}_{\text{abs}}),
\label{eq:abs-meas}
\end{equation}
optionally with an onboard inertial measurement (accelerometer/gyro) providing
the specific force used as a known input to the estimator's process model.
\emph{Relative} measurements relate the states of neighbouring agents; from
onboard relative sensing (vision, ultra-wideband ranging, radar) agent $i$
measures the relative position of each neighbour $j\in\Neigh_i$,
\begin{equation}
\vv{y}_{ij}^{\text{rel}}=\vv{p}_i-\vv{p}_j+\vv{n}_{ij}^{\text{rel}},\qquad
\vv{n}_{ij}^{\text{rel}}\sim\mathcal N(\vv{0},\mat{R}_{\text{rel}}).
\label{eq:rel-meas}
\end{equation}
The relative measurements are the coupling: they carry no absolute information by
themselves, but combined with a neighbour's absolute estimate they localise
agent $i$ without its own absolute fix.

\section{Local estimator and the translational reduction}

For the translational sub-problem---position and velocity driven by a measured
specific force with process noise---the model is linear, and by the
Kalman-filter reduction of \cref{sec:mhe} the local estimator is exactly an
information/covariance Kalman recursion. Writing the local state
$\vv{s}_i=(\vv{p}_i,\vv{v}_i)$ with the constant-acceleration kinematic model
\begin{equation}
\vv{s}_i^{+}=\mat{F}\vv{s}_i+\mat{G}\,\vv{a}_i^{\text{meas}}+\vv{q}_i,
\qquad
\mat{F}=\begin{bmatrix}\mat{I}&\Delta t\,\mat{I}\\\mat{0}&\mat{I}\end{bmatrix},
\quad
\mat{G}=\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac12\Delta t^2\mat{I}\\\Delta t\,\mat{I}\end{bmatrix},
\label{eq:dmhe-kin}
\end{equation}
the local update is the standard predict/correct pair with process covariance
$\mat{Q}_i$ and, when an absolute fix is available, the innovation update against
\cref{eq:abs-meas}. This recursion is numerically well conditioned from the first
sample---unlike a cold-started batch window---which is why we use it as the
per-agent engine; the full nonlinear estimator of \cref{sec:mhe} is the general
form of which this is the linear reduction.

\section{Relative fusion and the data-incest problem}
\label{sec:dmhe-fusion}

The distributed step fuses each relative measurement \cref{eq:rel-meas} as a
pseudo-measurement of $\vv{p}_i$ anchored on the neighbour's estimate: since
$\vv{p}_i=\vv{y}_{ij}^{\text{rel}}+\vv{p}_j$, the neighbour's estimate
$\est{\vv{p}}_j$ (with position covariance $\mat{P}_j$) supplies
\begin{equation}
\vv{z}_{ij}=\vv{y}_{ij}^{\text{rel}}+\est{\vv{p}}_j,\qquad
\text{covariance }\ \mat{R}_{\text{rel}}+\mat{P}_j,
\label{eq:pseudo-meas}
\end{equation}
which is folded into the local filter by an ordinary Kalman update. Inflating the
measurement covariance by the neighbour's own uncertainty $\mat{P}_j$ is what
makes the fusion \emph{covariance-aware}: a confident (well-localised) neighbour
is trusted, an uncertain one is discounted.

Naively fusing \cref{eq:pseudo-meas} for \emph{all} neighbours at every step
fails, for two reasons that the design must respect and that we observed directly
in simulation. First, \emph{data incest}: if two agents that are both uncertain
fuse each other's estimates, they reinforce a shared error and drift together---%
the classical rumour-propagation pathology of distributed estimation. Second,
\emph{time misalignment}: anchoring a window's past node to a neighbour's current
estimate injects an error that grows with the cluster's speed. We therefore adopt
a conservative, provably safe fusion policy:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item an agent uses relative fusion only \emph{when its own absolute fix is
        unavailable} (it otherwise behaves as an independent local filter,
        identical to the baseline);
  \item it anchors only to neighbours of \emph{strictly lower anchor level}.
        Define level~$0$ agents as those holding a current absolute fix; a
        denied agent with at least one anchored neighbour of level below $\ell$
        becomes anchored at level~$\ell$, fusing \cref{eq:pseudo-meas} against
        those neighbours' already-updated, same-step estimates with their
        (chain-inflated) covariances $\mat{P}_j$. The strict level ordering
        makes the information flow a directed acyclic graph, so estimates can
        never reinforce one another mutually---which is precisely what rules
        out incest. Capping the maximum level at $L{=}1$ recovers the
        conservative \emph{one-hop} policy (anchor only to absolutely-fixed
        neighbours); $L\ge2$ is \emph{multi-hop} anchoring, in which a denied
        agent with no absolutely-fixed neighbour still localises through a
        chain of anchored ones, at covariance appropriately grown along the
        chain; and
  \item the fusion is time-aligned---level-$\ell$ agents fuse in the
        $\ell$-th pass of a step, after all lower levels have completed, so
        every anchor is a fresh same-step estimate.
\end{enumerate}
The level assignment is purely topological (it depends only on which agents
hold a fix and on the graph, not on the estimates), so it is computed
identically by every implementation of the filter, and the fused
pseudo-measurement enters the local estimator through its ordinary measurement
channel---no solver modification is required. The condition for an agent to be
anchored at all is that the graph connect it to some absolutely-fixed agent
through a path of denied agents of length at most $L$; \cref{ch:simulation}
shows both the failure of the one-hop policy when that condition is violated at
$L{=}1$ and its recovery at $L{=}3$. This policy is a pragmatic instance of the
partition-based DMHE of Farina \etal
\cite{farina2010distributed,farina2012distributed}: neighbour states enter as
bounded, information-weighted pseudo-measurements, with the DAG ordering as the
incest guard. When richer coupling is warranted---all agents simultaneously
denied, or overlapping shared states---the arrival-cost consensus of
Battistelli \cite{battistelli2019distributed}, which fuses the local
information matrices $\Omega_i\leftarrow\sum_{j}W_{ij}\Omega_j$ over the graph
with the Metropolis weights of \cref{eq:metropolis}, is the general form of
which the leveled scheme is a structured special case; it restores
centralised-like stability at the price of a consensus loop per sample and is
compatible with the same interface.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Distributed MHE step at agent $i$ (translational reduction; the
one-hop $L{=}1$ instance---multi-hop anchoring repeats the fusion pass once per
level with anchors of strictly lower level)}
\label{alg:dmhe}
\DontPrintSemicolon
\KwIn{measured specific force $\vv{a}_i^{\text{meas}}$; absolute fix
$\vv{y}_i^{\text{abs}}$ if available; relative measurements
$\{\vv{y}_{ij}^{\text{rel}}\}_{j\in\Neigh_i}$; neighbour estimates
$\{(\est{\vv{s}}_j,\mat{P}_j)\}$}
\textbf{Predict:}\ \ $\est{\vv{s}}_i\!\leftarrow\!\mat{F}\est{\vv{s}}_i+\mat{G}\vv{a}_i^{\text{meas}}$,\ \
$\mat{P}_i\!\leftarrow\!\mat{F}\mat{P}_i\mat{F}\T+\mat{Q}_i$\;
\eIf{absolute fix available}{
  \textbf{Absolute update} against \cref{eq:abs-meas}\ \ (local filter; no relative fusion)\;
}{
  \ForEach{$j\in\Neigh_i$ \textbf{with} a current absolute fix}{
     $\vv{z}_{ij}\leftarrow\vv{y}_{ij}^{\text{rel}}+\est{\vv{p}}_j$;\ \
     Kalman update of $\est{\vv{s}}_i$ with covariance $\mat{R}_{\text{rel}}+\mat{P}_j$\;
  }
}
broadcast $(\est{\vv{s}}_i,\mat{P}_i)$ to $\Neigh_i$\;
\Return $\est{\vv{s}}_i$ to the controller
\end{algorithm}

\section{Stability and what is claimed}

The local estimator inherits the stability of the underlying moving-horizon
estimator (\cref{sec:mhe}); for the linear reduction it is the Kalman filter,
uniformly stable under standard detectability. The relative-fusion policy of
\cref{sec:dmhe-fusion} is a sequence of information-increasing Kalman updates
against uncorrelated anchors, so it cannot decrease certainty spuriously, and the
denied agent's error is bounded by the anchors' error plus the relative-%
measurement noise rather than growing without bound as in dead reckoning. The
partition-based convergence results of \cite{farina2010distributed} and the
consensus-stability results of \cite{battistelli2019distributed,schneider2017convergence}
provide the formal guarantees for the full nonlinear and all-denied cases; here
we claim, and demonstrate in \cref{ch:simulation}, that the policy keeps
absolute-denied agents localised to the anchor-plus-noise level through outages
that send an independent filter into unbounded drift.
